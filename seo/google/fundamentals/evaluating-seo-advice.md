# Evaluating SEO Advice

Google's guidance on evaluating third-party SEO tools, services, and advice.

## What Google says

### Evaluate external advice against official guidelines

There is plenty of third-party SEO advice on the internet. Some is helpful, some misrepresents how Google works. Good advice either:
- Qualifies claims as opinion based on data or experience
- Backs up claims by citing official Google Search guidance

### Think critically about third-party tools

Third-party SEO tools can be helpful, but:

- **Google doesn't evaluate or endorse third-party tools**
- These tools don't have access to Google's internal ranking data
- No tool can guarantee ranking success
- Be wary of tools that claim to be "approved" by Google

### Red flags

- Unsolicited emails claiming your site isn't listed in search engines
- Guarantees of #1 ranking
- Claims of a "special relationship" with Google
- Refusal to explain what changes they'll make
- Link popularity schemes

## What this means for this website

For a personal website, most third-party SEO tools are overkill. The essentials are:

1. **Google Search Console** (free, from Google directly): Monitor indexing, performance, and issues
2. **Rich Results Test** (free): Validate structured data
3. **URL Inspection Tool** (in Search Console): See how Google sees individual pages

Third-party tools like Ahrefs, SEMrush, or Moz can provide additional insights (keyword research, backlink analysis) but are not necessary for a personal site.

## Checklist

- [ ] Use Google Search Console as the primary monitoring tool
- [ ] Use Rich Results Test to validate structured data
- [ ] Be skeptical of unsolicited SEO advice
- [ ] Verify any third-party recommendations against Google's official documentation
